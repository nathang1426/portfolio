Lab 2

The 4 Ps Marketing Mix

Turning the brand into a plan: what we sell, what it costs, where you buy it, and how people hear about it.

ProductPricePlacePromotion

1What it is

Lab 2 lays out the Coastal Threads marketing mix. The core message ties straight back to the brand: premium feel without the premium price.

ProductDurable, comfortable flip-flops in five neutral colors with eco-friendly packaging
Price$39 a pair, or 2 for $70 (mix your colors)
PlaceDirect-to-consumer online, plus surf shops, college-town retailers, and pop-ups
PromotionInstagram, TikTok, and creator partnerships showing real-life moments

Pricing in the $35–45 range puts Coastal Threads in the affordable mid-tier: accessible for our 18–35 audience, but better quality than cheap alternatives. Price points like $39 and $44 stay under key psychological thresholds, and the bundle encourages people to buy more than one color.

2Process

  1. Started from the Lab 1 brand: affordable, quality, and laid-back.
  2. Looked at competitor prices to find a range that felt like a deal without seeming cheap.
  3. Chose where to sell: online first to keep prices low and control the brand, then local partners in beach and college towns.
  4. Planned promotion around where our audience already spends time, then designed the board and hero ad with AI-generated imagery.

The challenge

Pricing was the hardest part. Too low and the flip-flops would look cheap; too high and we'd lose the whole “premium feel without the premium price” promise.

How I solved it

I landed on $35–45 and used psychological price points ($39 and $44) plus a 2-for-$70 bundle, so it feels affordable and gives people a reason to buy a second pair.

3Reflection

What I learned

The 4 Ps all depend on each other. The price only works because selling direct online keeps costs down, and the promotion only works because it matches where the audience actually is.

What I'd improve

I'd add more detail on how the in-store partnerships would work and on how we'd measure whether the social promotion is actually driving sales.
