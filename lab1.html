Lab 1

Brand Foundations

For my advertising course, I created Coastal Threads, a fictional flip-flop brand. This first step defined who the brand is, who it's for, and why it wins.

Brand strategyAudience researchCompetitive analysisBrand board
"Coastal living shouldn't come with a high price."

1What it is

Lab 1 is the foundation of my brand. Coastal Threads makes affordable, comfortable flip-flops and sandals designed to look and feel like the big-name brands at a fraction of the price. Its values are simple: comfort and style while staying true to our roots.

CategoryFootwear: flip-flops & sandals
PersonalityRelaxed, authentic, timeless, accessible
Audience18–35: beach lovers, students, surfers, travelers
Voice"We talk like a local, not a billboard."

Our equities: why we win

Our biggest strength is offering the classic look and comfort people expect from premium flip-flop brands without the premium price. Instead of paying extra for a big name, customers get a timeless coastal style that still looks and feels high quality, without spending $70 or more.

The competition

Rainbow Sandals built its reputation on durable leather sandals and a strong connection to surf culture. OluKai focuses on premium comfort and a Hawaiian-inspired lifestyle. Both have strong reputations, but Coastal Threads competes by giving customers a similar classic style and quality at a much more affordable price.

2Process

  1. Wrote out the brand basics: what we sell, what we stand for, and the problem we solve (overpriced flip-flops).
  2. Researched the two biggest competitors, Rainbow and OluKai, to understand why people choose them.
  3. Defined our target audience and narrowed it down to a clear core group.
  4. Built the visual identity (logo, color palette, imagery, and voice) and pulled it all into one brand board, using AI tools to generate the product imagery.

The challenge

My first version of the target audience was too broad. I described it as anyone from kids to adults who likes the beach, which made it hard to picture one real customer.

How I solved it

I focused on who would care most about getting premium style at a lower price and narrowed the core audience to 18–35 year olds: students, travelers, and surfers who value comfort and simplicity.

3Reflection

What I learned

A brand is more than a logo. Every choice, from colors to voice to price, has to point back to the same idea. Studying competitors also showed me that a brand needs a clear reason to exist next to the big names.

What I'd improve

I'd do more real research on the audience, like a short survey of students about what they pay for flip-flops and why, so the positioning is backed up by actual data.
